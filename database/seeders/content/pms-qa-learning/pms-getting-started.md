## Overview

The Project Management System (PMS) is the team's shared place for projects, requirements, enhancements, production issues, Quality Assurance Tickets (QAT), cycles, releases and discussion. It helps people see why work exists, who is responsible, what has changed, and what needs attention next. Your account and project membership determine which information and actions you can use.

## Who Should Read This

Every new project team member, especially QA Engineers joining an existing project. Managers can use it as an onboarding checklist.

## Learning Objectives

- Access PMS and join the correct project.
- Find project context, assigned tickets and knowledge articles.
- Understand why a visible page may have different available actions for different roles.
- Keep profile and assignment information current.

## Key Concepts Explained

An **account** identifies you in PMS. A **project membership** grants access to a particular project's information; having an account alone does not assign you to every project. A **project role** such as QA Engineer, Developer, Business Analyst (BA) or Project Manager (PM) controls what you may do there. A **ticket assignment** identifies who handles a specific item and is separate from project membership. The dashboard and ticket lists gather work across projects you can access.

PMS has ordinary registration and invitation-based entry. An ordinary registration may wait for administrator approval before the profile is active. A valid project invitation can activate an eligible invited account and add it to the intended project, subject to the invitation's conditions. Existing users can accept an invitation. Do not share an invitation link casually; ask the PM or administrator for a new one if it has expired.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. Open the PMS login page from the back-office project management area. Sign in with your own account. If you do not have one, use registration or the project invitation supplied by an authorised team member.
2. If you registered normally, check whether your account needs approval. If you have a project invitation, follow the link and complete its acceptance steps with the correct account.
3. Open **Projects** and confirm the assigned project appears. If it does not, ask the project manager to check membership; creating a second account usually makes the problem worse.
4. Open the project and read its description, objectives, membership, cycles, releases and linked work. Confirm that the project name and key match your assignment.
5. Open the ticket list or dashboard to find work assigned to you. Filter by project and your QA assignment where useful. Read the full ticket, its linked requirements and recent comments before updating it.
6. Open **Knowledge Sharing** and search by a topic or category. Project-specific articles are available only when your project membership permits them.
7. Review your profile details and job information. Keep contact and role-related data accurate; ask an administrator or PM when a role or permission is wrong.

## Real-World PGOS Example

Imagine a new QA Engineer invited to the Personal Goal Operating System (PGOS) project. After accepting the valid invitation and signing in, they verify PGOS in the project list. They read the project description and the goal-related requirement ticket. Next they read the PGOS overview and testing lessons in Knowledge Sharing. They find a QAT item assigned to them or, if their role permits, create a new QAT item tied to PGOS. They record test evidence and check comments for recent developer changes. If PGOS is missing, they report the access problem to the PM before attempting to test or file tickets in another project.

The project view gives shared context, while the ticket view gives the exact work item. A dashboard count is a prompt to investigate, not a substitute for reading acceptance criteria and history.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming registration automatically grants access to PGOS.
- Using another person's login or creating duplicate accounts to bypass an approval delay.
- Selecting a similarly named project without confirming its key.
- Changing a ticket state before reading its current owner, required fields and dependencies.
- Treating an unavailable button as a product defect before checking your project role.

## Best Practices

Use one personal account, verify each invitation, and keep the project key visible in your notes. Ask for the smallest role needed to perform your assigned work. Save useful knowledge articles and read their last-updated date; if a lesson conflicts with a current ticket or policy, ask the BA or PM and propose an article correction.

## QA Checklist and Key Takeaways

- [ ] My account is active and I can sign in.
- [ ] PGOS appears in my assigned projects.
- [ ] I know where to find requirements, my QAT work and comments.
- [ ] I can find PGOS Knowledge Sharing articles.
